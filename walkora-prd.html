Section 1

Overview

Walkora is a privacy-conscious social walking and activity-tracking application that helps people build sustainable walking habits through automatic step tracking, flexible goals, meaningful progress insights, supportive friendships, group challenges, and positive achievement recognition. It is not just a pedometer: its core value is the combination of reliable tracking, personal motivation, social accountability, accessible progress reporting, and user-controlled privacy.

Walkora helps people build sustainable walking habits by combining accurate activity tracking, flexible personal goals, meaningful progress insights, friendly challenges, and privacy-controlled social accountability.

Vision

Make everyday walking easier to sustain by transforming activity data into achievable goals, visible progress, and supportive shared experiences.

Mission

Help people move more consistently — without shame, unhealthy pressure, or unnecessary complexity.

Promise

Encouraging rather than demanding. Social rather than intrusive. Competitive without becoming unhealthy. Useful before registration.

Defensible value

Android and wearable platforms already show basic step totals. Walkora wins on what they don't do:

  • Flexible goals with rest days and recovery modes
  • Positive, opt-in social accountability
  • Fair challenge models beyond absolute step totals
  • Cross-device activity consolidation via Health Connect
  • Excellent progress storytelling and recaps
  • Strong privacy and social-safety controls from day one
  • A polished, fully accessible experience
  • Genuinely useful guest mode — value before sign-in
Section 2

Problem statement

Eight validated problems drive the product. Every requirement in §7 traces back to at least one of them.

P1Motivation fades

Pedometers show totals without a reason to return. Users need encouragement, achievable goals, visible progress, and optional social accountability to build a habit loop.

P2Fragmented data

Steps live across phone sensors, watches, Health Connect, Samsung Health, Garmin, Fitbit, Xiaomi, and Oura. Users need one reliable view with transparent sources, sync status, and dedup.

P3Rigid goals punish

One fixed target ignores ability, routine, and recovery. Missing one day breaks streaks and feels punitive. Users need flexible goals, rest days, and pause options.

P4Walking feels isolated

Solo tracking gives weak accountability. Friends, private groups, fair challenges, and mutual encouragement keep people engaged.

P5Raw totals lack context

A number doesn't say whether the goal was reached, whether you're improving, or whether the data is even current. Users need trends, comparisons, records, and goal progress.

P6Leaderboards discourage

Absolute step rankings favour already-active users and demoralize beginners. Fair modes — goal %, consistency, improvement, team average — are required.

P7Social needs safety

Friends, groups, and chat invite unwanted contact, harassment, and spam without blocking, reporting, moderation, rate limits, and age-appropriate protections from the first social release.

P8Privacy is expected

Activity history and social graphs are personal. Users need precise control over discovery, visibility, leaderboard presence, connected devices, notifications, export, and deletion.

Section 3

Goals & non-goals

User objectives

  • Make daily walking progress easy to understand
  • Help users develop consistent routines
  • Reduce friction during setup and synchronization
  • Provide useful guest functionality before registration
  • Support both private tracking and social participation
  • Make competition inclusive and configurable
  • Give users clear ownership of their data and privacy

Business objectives

  • Strong daily/weekly retention through genuine user value
  • Organic growth through invitations and privacy-safe sharing
  • Differentiation through fair competition and flexible goals
  • Trust through transparent data practices
  • A path to group and organizational wellness programs

Non-goals (initial release)

The initial release will not:

  • Provide medical diagnosis or treatment recommendations
  • Require an account for basic activity tracking
  • Promote extreme step targets or unsafe overexertion
  • Rank users only by absolute step totals
  • Depend on continuous GPS for basic step counting
  • Promise direct integrations before provider validation
  • Include location-based routes without explicit consent and a separate privacy review

Inclusive product principle

Walkora must support different routines and abilities without implying that higher step totals represent greater worth, effort, or health.

Section 4

Target users & personas

AAmara, 34 — the restarter

Beginning a daily movement routine after a long break. Wants realistic targets and zero shame. Needs: suggested starting goals, rest days, recovery mode, non-punitive streaks, private-by-default profile.

BDele & friends — the accountability crew

Mixed iPhone/Android friend group with very different fitness levels. Needs: cross-platform invites, fair ranking by goal % and consistency, encouragement chat, mute/hide controls.

CThe Okafor family — the private group

Three generations in one private walking group. Needs: invite-only groups, simple group goals, safe moderated chat, age-appropriate protections, large-text support.

DPriya, 41 — the wearable power user

Garmin owner who wants one consolidated view plus a social layer her watch lacks. Needs: Health Connect sync, source priority, dedup, last-sync transparency, data export.

Secondary audiences

Organizations running voluntary wellness initiatives, schools and youth programs using privacy-safe group challenges, community coordinators, and accessibility-focused walking groups. These inform later-phase requirements (team challenges, organization groups) but do not gate the MVP.

Section 8

Information architecture & navigation

The reference product leans on small icon-only top controls. Walkora replaces this with a labelled Material 3 bottom navigation bar of five destinations. Notifications stay reachable from the top app bar.

DestinationPurposeAvailable to guests
TodayPersonal steps, goal progress ring, daily summary, sync status✓
ProgressHistory, trends, records, streaks, goal-completion calendar✓
CommunityFriends, groups, leaderboards, challengesPreview + contextual sign-in
MessagesDirect, group, and challenge conversationsPreview + contextual sign-in
ProfileAchievements, settings, devices, privacy, accountPartial (local settings)

Navigation principles

  • Labels always accompany primary navigation icons
  • User state is preserved when switching destinations
  • Core actions reachable in one or two taps
  • The Today screen is never overloaded with social features
  • Contextual actions create groups, challenges, and invitations
  • Back navigation is predictable everywhere
  • Deep links for invitations, challenges, messages, achievements
  • Icon-only controls reserved for familiar secondary actions

Section 10

Core user journeys

J1 · First-time tracking

  1. Open WalkoraBranded welcome states the value proposition.
  2. Understand trackingPlain-language explanation of what will be tracked and why.
  3. Grant activity permissionContextual rationale first; decline path explained (Mock 1).
  4. Confirm data sourcePhone sensor or Health Connect, with status confirmation.
  5. Set goal & rest daysSuggested ranges; direct numeric entry.
  6. Optional notificationsRequested in context, never on first launch.
  7. Tracking confirmed → TodayFirst sync confirmation lands the user on the dashboard.

J3 · Friend & leaderboard

  1. Open CommunitySearch username, scan QR, or open an invite link.
  2. Review profile & privacyWhat the other person will and won't see.
  3. Send request → acceptBoth appear per their own visibility settings.
  4. Pick period & ranking modeGoal %, steps, consistency, improvement.
  5. Stay in controlEncourage, mute, hide, remove, block, or report at any point.

J5 · Wearable sync

  1. Open Connected devicesHealth Connect first; providers behind validation flags.
  2. Review & grant permissionsPer-data-type explanation.
  3. Import & dedupeChosen date range; conflicts explained.
  4. Verify statusConnection state and last-sync time visible; change priority, refresh, disconnect, or delete imports anytime.

J2 · Guest-to-account conversion

  1. Track as a guestFull local tracking, goals, history — no account.
  2. Touch a social/sync featureWalkora explains the concrete benefit of registering.
  3. AuthenticateGoogle or email; verification completes.
  4. History migratesEligible local history is associated with the account — nothing lost.
  5. Set social privacyPrivate-by-default; user opts into visibility.
  6. ContinueUser lands back in the feature that prompted sign-in.

J4 · Group challenge

  1. Create or join a groupInvite-only by default.
  2. Admin creates a challengeTarget, duration, ranking mode, and rules visible before joining.
  3. Members join or declineParticipation is always opt-in.
  4. Track & encourageLive standings plus respectful group chat.
  5. Fair-play checksSuspicious records flagged before results are affected.
  6. Recap & celebrateCompletion summary, badges, share, or rematch.

Journey acceptance rule

A journey ships only when every step has defined loading, empty, error, offline, and permission-denied states, TalkBack coverage, and analytics events approved under the consent model (§19).

Section 12

Accessibility requirements

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA where applicable, plus current Android accessibility guidance. The reference product showed teal-on-black contrast risks, tiny grey text, colour-only tab selection, and dense icon-only controls — all excluded by the requirements below.

  • Descriptive TalkBack labels on every interactive element
  • Logical focus order across all screens and dialogs
  • Switch Access support
  • Minimum touch-target sizes met everywhere
  • Android font scaling without truncation; layouts reflow
  • Landscape and responsive reflow supported
  • Visible focus indicators where relevant
  • Reduced-motion preferences respected (incl. celebrations)
  • Important state changes announced accessibly
  • Text summaries accompany every chart
  • No colour-only meaning — icons, labels, patterns, values
  • Contrast validated in light, dark, and high-contrast modes
  • Accessible form labels, instructions, validation, recovery
  • Leaderboards and dense lists reflow with large text

Verification limitation

Screenshots cannot confirm TalkBack behaviour, focus order, dynamic text scaling, keyboard access, motion behaviour, live-region announcements, chart semantics, or runtime contrast. These are verified in the implemented application — automated checks in CI plus assistive-technology test passes per release.

Section 13

Privacy, security & trust

Privacy by default and data minimization are architectural requirements, not settings. The application must never rely on client-side authorization alone.

Data protection

Explicit consent for health and contact data · purpose limitation · encrypted transport · secure local storage for sensitive values · retention policies · consent history · data export · account and associated-data deletion · clear privacy notices.

Platform security

Secure authentication · server-side authorization for every protected operation · session revocation · rate limiting · secret management · dependency and vulnerability monitoring · protection against account enumeration · input validation and output encoding · safe file-upload controls for profile and group images.

Community safety

Abuse prevention · message spam controls · blocking and reporting on every social surface · moderation and appeals workflow · least-privilege, audited administrative access · suspicious-activity detection for challenge fairness · age-appropriate protections.

Section 14

Data quality & fairness

Trust in the number is the product. Users must always be able to see which source contributed to a total and when it was last updated.

The engine must handle

  • Multiple simultaneous activity sources
  • Duplicate records across phone + wearable
  • Delayed synchronization
  • Time-zone and daylight-saving changes
  • Device restarts and manual clock changes
  • Offline periods with later reconciliation
  • Sensor limitations and gaps

Fairness controls

  • Suspicious or impossible values flagged for review
  • Challenge eligibility windows enforced
  • Corrections and dispute handling workflow
  • Source attribution on every displayed total
  • Flagged data excluded from boards until resolved
Section 15

Notification & engagement principles

Engagement must prioritize user value over notification volume. Every notification Walkora sends is:

  • Useful — tied to a real event or recoverable action
  • Configurable per category
  • Respectful of quiet hours
  • Rate limited
  • Privacy aware (lock-screen previews controllable)
  • Free from shame-based language
  • Clear about the triggering event
  • Actionable when recovery is possible

Users can disable all social notifications while retaining activity and security notifications — the categories are architecturally separate (NTF-01).

Section 20

Assumptions & dependencies

  • The initial product is Android-first
  • Health Connect is the preferred aggregation layer where supported
  • Basic tracking remains available without an account
  • Social features require a registered account
  • Messaging requires moderation and abuse controls before launch
  • Provider integrations require technical, policy, and commercial validation
  • Energy estimates are informational, never central to positioning
  • Location tracking is outside the initial scope
  • Final visuals use a distinct Walkora design system, not the reference product's
Section 21

Final scope statement

Walkora will deliver a reliable, accessible, and privacy-conscious Android walking experience that works for private users and grows naturally into optional social participation. The initial product must establish excellent tracking, goals, history, synchronization, guest access, and user trust before adding the operational complexity of friends, groups, challenges, and messaging.

This sequencing protects product quality while ensuring that every later social feature is built on accurate activity data, clear privacy controls, and a safe community foundation.
